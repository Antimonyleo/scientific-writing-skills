# Hypotheses, mechanisms, and grant aims

## Start from what is actually known

Separate observations from their interpretation. Identify the system, preparation, conditions, measurement, and uncertainty. If only a broad objective is supplied, state the assumptions needed to turn it into a research question.

An observation can motivate several mechanisms. Specify how each mechanism produces the observation and what it predicts under a perturbation. A renamed observation is not an explanation. Include a measurement artifact or ordinary alternative when it is plausible, rather than constructing only ambitious mechanisms.

For a candidate, use a compact structure:

- Question or design objective.
- Mechanism or design principle.
- Key assumption and evidence supporting it, or an unchecked-premise label.
- Prediction under an informative intervention or comparison.
- Competing explanation and differentiating observation.
- Minimal next test and its value under either outcome.

Use only fields that clarify the task. Do not force a causal hypothesis onto a descriptive map, an exploratory resource, or an engineering benchmark.

## Make predictions discriminate

State what should change, in which direction or regime, and what comparison is needed. Quantitative thresholds require a basis such as pilot data, a physical model, measurement resolution, or a user-defined target. Otherwise offer qualitative predictions and identify how to calibrate a threshold.

For nucleic-acid or molecular systems, consider concentration, stoichiometry, salt, temperature, sequence and length, topology, folding history, timescale, aggregation, and assay sensitivity where they could alter the conclusion. Use the smallest relevant subset; do not list every possible variable.

For computational biology, distinguish a biological mechanism from a model's representational assumption. A successful fit may be compatible with several mechanisms or parameter sets. Identify an independent observable, perturbation, or held-out condition that distinguishes them.

## Convert a direction into an aim

Express the knowledge or capability to be gained, the rationale, approach, expected interpretable outcomes, major dependency, risk, and alternative. Tie the aim to an actual grant's criteria when available; writing rules come from that sponsor, not from this template.

An aim should have a scientific outcome beyond completing a task. Related aims may depend on one another, but identify what later work can still establish if an earlier result is negative or the preferred technique fails. Avoid inventing preliminary evidence or promising every candidate will succeed.

Describe novelty relative to checked prior work, with a search boundary. For each option, name the closest prior approach found and what differs; if none was checked, say so and offer a bounded literature check before the novelty claim is relied on. The ability to articulate a hypothesis does not establish that it is new or feasible.
