# Grant development and review

## Establish the governing call

Read the active solicitation, amendments, application instructions, assessment criteria, and any relevant program-officer instructions supplied by the user. Record the opportunity and version or access date when checking requirements. Confirm the document's stage: inquiry, invited proposal, full application, fellowship, or resubmission.

For a requirement review, build a compact table:

| Requirement or criterion | Official source and section | Draft location | Status or action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Keep mandatory requirements, sponsor preferences, and your editorial suggestions distinct. Verify eligibility, deadlines and time zones, limits, required attachments, permitted assistance, and submission route when relevant to the task. If current instructions are unavailable, complete a bounded scientific or writing review and identify unresolved requirements. Do not certify compliance from an old call or a generic agency summary.

Use the sponsor's own framework. NIH, NSF, foundations, and fellowships need not use the same headings, criteria, or proposal stage. Do not import an NSF broader-impacts requirement into a foundation proposal. Check the current applicable AI-assistance policy before undertaking a governed drafting task; when it limits assistance, work within the permitted review or editing scope.

## Make the proposed science assessable

Connect the important problem, precise gap, objective, rationale, planned work, expected knowledge or capability, and evidence of feasibility. A hypothesis is useful for a mechanistic study; a method, resource, or infrastructure proposal may need a design objective and validation criteria instead. Do not force every proposal into a hypothesis template.

For each aim or work package, identify:

- The question or objective and why solving it matters.
- The approach and readout, with controls and comparisons that can discriminate among outcomes.
- What is established, what preliminary work supports, and what remains to be done.
- The measurable outcome and a justified success criterion. Suggested thresholds remain provisional until supported by evidence, benchmarks, or the applicant's decision.
- Dependencies, main technical risk, early warning signal, and a feasible alternative.
- The knowledge retained if the favored hypothesis is unsupported or the preferred method fails.

Distinguish dependencies from avoidable fragility: later work can depend on earlier work, but the proposal should explain what survives failure and how an alternative restores a path forward. Do not claim all aims must always be independent.

Check that effort, expertise, facilities, budget, duration, deliverables, and milestones describe the same project. A milestone is an assessable result or decision, not merely “perform experiments.” Never invent access to equipment, collaborator agreement, costs, preliminary results, or success rates.

## Strengthen persuasion with evidence

Specify what is different from verified prior work and why it changes what can be learned or done. Separate deliverables from downstream outcomes and applications. Avoid promising impact that the proposed measurement cannot establish.

For resubmissions, connect each material prior criticism to a revision, evidence, or reasoned explanation. Follow the sponsor's response format; a journal-style response letter is not a universal grant requirement.

For a review, return the most decision-relevant scientific and fit concerns before copyedits. For an authorized rewrite, preserve the applicant's scientific choices and identify substantive proposals for their review.

## Final check

Reconcile aims, summary, approach, timeline, budget narrative, and expected outcomes. Inspect exported documents when checking page limits or legibility. Finish the requested drafting or review without treating it as authorization to submit the application or contact a sponsor.

## Current source starting points

- [NIH application-section advice](https://www.grants.nih.gov/grants-process/write-application/advice-on-application-sections): goals, expected outcomes, and feasibility; consult the actual opportunity for requirements.
- [NSF merit review](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/merit-review): agency review framework; verify solicitation-specific criteria.
- [Sloan application guidance](https://sloan.org/grants/apply): foundation process, program fit, outputs, and milestones; program instructions may add requirements.
