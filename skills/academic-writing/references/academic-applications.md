# Academic applications

Use the shared method and only the branch relevant to the requested application or document. Writing, substantive revision, and light polishing use the same evidence boundaries with different intervention depths.

## Shared method

Read the actual call or posting and identify the reader's decision, required documents, criteria, and limits. Check current eligibility or assistance rules only when they govern the task. Distinguish mandatory requirements from conventions and editorial suggestions. Identify conflicting instructions rather than silently selecting a deadline or format. Complete the supported drafting when other information is missing.

Connect each important criterion to a verified experience, contribution, result, or proposed plan. A small evidence table can help a package; it is unnecessary for a sentence edit. Preserve the applicant's role, degree/job dates, publication and acceptance status, grants, teaching record, and confirmed commitments. Distinguish personal contributions from team achievements and future intentions from demonstrated outcomes. Do not turn publication counts, prestige, or unsupported impact adjectives into evidence of merit.

Adapt framing to the audience while preserving the facts and voice. Across documents, keep the same record and coherent direction without repeating the same paragraphs. Mark missing evidence separately. Do not invent hardship, identity, recommendation testimony, metrics, collaborators' agreement, or institutional resources. Required personal context comes from the applicant's own account.

## Awards, nominations, and fellowships

For an achievement award, explain the specific contribution, evidence of its significance, and how it meets the award's criteria. Select relevant accomplishments rather than replacing the statement with a publication list or an unsolicited project proposal. Preserve accepted, published, submitted, and preprint distinctions.

For a fellowship, distinguish the candidate's preparation and development from the project rationale and feasibility. Explain what the applicant will learn, how the proposed environment could support that learning, and how it advances their trajectory. Host fit is not host consent: describe unconfirmed mentorship, facilities access, or training as proposed. Use the grant reference when a substantial research plan is requested; do not copy one funder's headings into every fellowship.

When drafting nomination or support materials, identify the intended speaker. An applicant evidence packet or proposed letter scaffold must not impersonate a nominator's private judgment, first-hand observations, or endorsement. Use only supplied, attributable facts; the actual recommender owns their final account. Respect applicant-authorship and permitted-assistance requirements in the governing call.

## Conferences and travel support

Identify whether the request is for a research abstract, participation statement, travel award, session/workshop proposal, or a combined package; these answer different questions.

- Abstract: communicate the question, approach, actual finding or explicitly proposed work, and supported implication. Match the audience and the call's treatment of completed versus prospective research.
- Participation or travel statement: explain the applicant's contribution, concrete learning or feedback sought, and why this meeting advances the work or career. Include financial need, costs, or eligibility facts only when supplied and requested; flag missing support without inventing it.
- Session/workshop proposal: connect theme and audience to scientific value, format, and proposed contributions. Distinguish proposed speakers or partners from confirmed participants.

Preserve submission, acceptance, invitation, presenting-author, and award statuses. Reconcile the abstract, CV, bio, and application answers where supplied. Count each constrained field separately using the stated convention; verify the form's count if it treats markup or symbols differently.

## Academic job applications

Use the actual position and institutional mission. A teaching-focused role, research-intensive faculty search, postdoctoral appointment, and staff-scientist position need different emphasis.

| Document | Its job |
| --- | --- |
| Cover letter | Connect the strongest relevant evidence and future contribution to this position; orient the reader to the package |
| Research statement | Explain the scientific direction, actual personal contribution, and feasible future program appropriate to the role; distinguish intellectual independence from unsupported PI or funding claims |
| Teaching/mentoring statement | Connect learning goals to actual practices and available evidence or reflection; clearly label proposed future teaching |
| CV or biosketch | Present a consistent, accurately labelled record in the requested format |
| Service or other requested statement | Address the stated criterion through actual actions and concrete plans without inventing experience or requesting unnecessary personal disclosure |

For limited teaching experience, use genuine TA work, mentoring, or other relevant experience with its correct scope. Do not fabricate instructor-of-record roles, evaluations, student outcomes, or causal teaching effectiveness. Describe future courses, mentoring, and collaborations as proposals unless confirmed. Verify any named program, person, or facility used to make an institutional-fit claim.

Check dates, roles, publication status, research direction, and commitments across the requested package. Do not impose a universal page count, course list, statement requirement, or career template.
