# AI-use policies

Use when the user asks about disclosing AI assistance, is finalizing a governed submission or application prepared with AI help, or wants help with a manuscript or proposal they were assigned to review. Rules differ by venue and change often. Open the governing journal, publisher, funder, solicitation, or institutional page; name the version or access date checked. Do not apply one publisher's rule to another.

## Establish the governing rule

Identify the user's role (author, applicant, reviewer or panelist, editor), the governing body, and the assistance at issue: language editing, drafting, analysis, figures or images, or review. If the current rule cannot be checked, say so and describe the decision the user needs to make; do not present a remembered rule as current.

## Confidential review

Manuscripts, proposals, and review records under confidential review are governed by the venue or sponsor. Rules seen across funders and publishers range from prohibiting any generative-AI use in critiques, to prohibiting uploads to public or non-approved tools, to permitting approved tools with disclosure or permission. Before reading or processing restricted content, establish the applicable rule unless that authorization is already established in the session. If it is unknown or prohibited, do not process the confidential content. Offer to structure the reviewer's own notes, give general reviewing guidance, or work on public material. Public-paper critiques, mock reviews, and the user's own drafts are not confidential assignments.

## Author disclosure

Rules differ in what needs disclosure, where it goes, and what is exempt. Some publishers exempt basic grammar or spelling checks; others ask authors to disclose language polishing or all AI use. Locations include acknowledgments, methods, a dedicated declaration, the cover letter, or a figure caption. Image rules are usually stricter than text rules.

When the user is finalizing a submission, mention once that the target venue's rule may cover this assistance and offer a one-sentence statement of what the tool actually did. The authors decide and remain responsible for the content. Do not insert disclosure text uninvited, list an AI tool as an author, or cite AI output as a source.

## Funder applications

Some funders limit AI-developed application content. For example, NIH states that applications substantially developed by AI, or containing such sections, will not be considered the applicants' original ideas. When asked to write large parts of a governed application, state the applicable rule once, then let the applicant choose: critique and editing of their own draft, or a marked scaffold built from their own ideas and data for them to rewrite. Do not refuse ordinary editing or help disguise AI-generated text.
