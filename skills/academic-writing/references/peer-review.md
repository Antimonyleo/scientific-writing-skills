# Giving a paper or proposal review

Use for a scientific critique, a referee or panel report, or editing an existing reviewer report. Answering comments as an author belongs to the reviewer-response reference. For a prose-only edit of an existing report, preserve the reviewer's judgment and supplied recommendation; do not perform a new merits assessment unless requested.

## Establish the review basis

Identify the artifact, review purpose, stage, available materials, criteria, and requested output. Public-paper critique and an internal mock review do not imply a formal confidential assignment. For assigned reviews, follow the actual venue or sponsor's confidentiality, assistance, conflict, and competence requirements ([AI-use policies](ai-use-policies.md)); resolve an unknown restriction before processing restricted material. Do not repeat an authorization already established in the session. Reading a file into a hosted assistant is external processing, even when the file is stored locally.

Inspect only available evidence and state material limits, such as a missing supplement, raw data, or specialist method. Do not equate “not provided for this review” with “does not exist” or “was not performed.”

## Assess against the actual criteria

For a paper, identify its contribution and whether the design, analysis, displays, and available evidence support the claims. Use the scientific-review reference for detailed appraisal. Distinguish validity, reporting completeness, novelty or importance where required, and presentation. A soundness-focused venue need not require a transformative result.

For a proposal, assess the prospective question, rationale, approach, controls, feasibility, dependencies, milestones, outputs, risks, and alternatives against the supplied call. Use the grant reference when its opportunity-specific checks are needed. Do not demand completed results or preliminary data that the mechanism does not require, or judge proposed work as if it had already succeeded.

Recognize supported strengths. For each material concern, give the claim or location, observable problem, implication, and smallest sufficient remedy. Mark questions and conditional concerns as such. Recommend new evidence only when necessary for the retained claim; distinguish that requirement from optional extensions or personal preferences. Do not demand citations for prestige, self-promotion, or a predetermined narrative.

Prioritize concerns affecting the central conclusion or feasibility, then interpretation and reproducibility, then minor presentation. Do not generate a fixed quota of criticisms, infer misconduct or AI authorship from prose, or use checklist counts as a scientific-quality score.

## Produce a usable report

Follow the requested form. When no form is supplied, a concise contribution summary, strengths, major concerns, and minor points is a useful starting shape, not a mandatory set of headings.

Keep the scientific issues the authors need to address visible in author-facing comments. Add confidential editor or panel notes only when requested and permitted; some venues restrict them. Keep any recommendation consistent with the substantive concerns, and place sensitive process or ethics questions in the appropriate permitted channel. Do not hide the rationale for adverse scientific criticism from the authors.

Give an advisory recommendation or panel rating when the user or form requests it. Use the supplied scale and explain the evidence and uncertainty; if no scale was supplied, use a qualitative assessment rather than inventing a numerical grade. Distinguish a supported reviewer recommendation from the editor's or funder's actual decision. An assistance task does not authorize submission or correspondence.

## Re-review and final check

Compare prior concerns, responses, and actual revisions when supplied. Distinguish resolved, partial, open, new, and unverifiable issues. Recheck changed material and consequential dependencies. Do not repeatedly impose a preference already addressed, but do not suppress a newly discovered validity problem merely because the relevant text is unchanged.

Check that quotations, locators, factual statements, and requests match the available artifact. State what was reviewed and what was not reproduced. The accountable reviewer retains final expert judgment and responsibility for the report.

## Source starting points

[COPE's reviewer guidelines](https://doi.org/10.24318/cope.2019.1.9) address responsible review. [PLOS reviewer guidance](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/reviewer-guidelines) illustrates venue-specific criteria and comment channels; it is not a template for every journal or panel.
