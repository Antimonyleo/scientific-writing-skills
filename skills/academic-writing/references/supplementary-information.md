# Supplementary information

Use for writing, polishing, organizing, or checking supplementary methods, notes, figures, tables, sequences, derivations, movies, datasets, and code documentation. Apply the requested depth: a caption edit needs caption and evidence checks, not an audit of every companion file.

## Establish the package

Use the journal's current distinction among main text, Methods, Extended Data, supplementary information (SI), and repository deposits. Numbering, references, captions, file types, size limits, and required inventories vary. Follow supplied instructions; do not turn another publisher's convention into a requirement.

For a substantial package, map each item to its purpose, main-text callout, file, and underlying result or method. Identify duplicates, missing items, and obsolete versions. Keep the main scientific argument and caveats assessable in the main article; use SI for supporting depth rather than hiding a central unsupported inference or an unfavorable result.

## Make supporting evidence interpretable

- Methods and protocols: report the actual material, preparation, conditions, procedures, independent unit, controls, exclusions, and analysis details needed for the relevant result. For sequence or molecular designs, preserve identities, orientations, modifications, units, and mapping to samples. For nucleic-acid nanostructures, check for machine-readable strand sequences, scaffold source, buffer and cation concentrations, strand excess, annealing protocol, purification, how yield was quantified, and imaging or gel conditions. For nanomaterial–biology experiments, minimum-information reporting such as [MIRIBEL](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41565-018-0246-4) can identify missing material, biological-system, and protocol details. Flag missing parameters instead of reconstructing customary laboratory practice.
- Computational work: state the relevant input provenance, transformations, parameters, versions, seeds where applicable, and how outputs connect to the figures or tables. Differentiate an algorithm description, runnable code, and an actual reproduction.
- Figures and tables: use unique identifiers, stable panel labels, units, sample definitions, uncertainty definitions, and legends that explain the evidence without requiring guesswork. Do not treat many images or particles as independent preparations.
- Derivations and notes: define notation and assumptions and connect the conclusion to its use in the main text. Check symbols and units across artifacts.
- Data and code: distinguish deposited, publicly accessible, restricted, planned, and unavailable material. Verify links, release/accession identifiers, file contents, and access when claiming availability; a statement or untested URL alone is insufficient. Supply machine-readable values when appropriate or required rather than presenting an image of a numeric table as reusable data.

These are relevant checks, not a demand to add every field to every supplement.

## Reconcile the article and SI

Trace central claims to their supporting item and check that reported values, conditions, preparation counts, definitions, exclusions, and statistical interpretations agree. A conflict is unresolved until checked against authoritative records; do not silently choose one version or manufacture missing evidence.

Check that callouts resolve to existing items, numbering and filenames agree, captions describe the correct content, references resolve under the venue's rules, and removed items leave no dangling callouts. Propagate an approved substantive change to all affected artifacts, including a response letter when in scope.

After file edits, compile or export the affected documents and inspect relevant pages, legends, tables, and links. Check actual companion files before claiming a complete package. Report missing records or items explicitly; organization and polished prose cannot validate an unsupported control or repair an unknown protocol.

## Source starting point

[PLOS supporting-information guidance](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/supporting-information) illustrates that caption, filename, and packaging instructions are publisher-specific. Check the actual target venue rather than applying these examples universally.
