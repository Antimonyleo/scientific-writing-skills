# Evidence synthesis and research gaps

## Extract evidence at the right level

For a substantial synthesis, maintain enough information to check a claim:

| Source and locator | System and conditions | Design, comparator, and independent unit | Finding and uncertainty | Limitation and relevance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Record whether the relevant content was examined in the full text, abstract, or another supplied artifact. Keep observation separate from mechanism proposed by the authors. Describe missing uncertainty as unreported; do not reconstruct an error bar from a figure without an explicit, disclosed extraction method.

Follow related preprints, conference papers, journal versions, and follow-ups. Shared datasets or experiments create dependent evidence even when paper titles differ. A review citing several papers does not supply independent replication if all trace to one experiment.

## Explain agreement and disagreement

Compare the actual systems, preparation, concentration, temperature, ionic conditions, measurement, endpoint, scale, comparator, analysis, and model assumptions that could affect the finding. Do not combine results simply because authors use the same term.

Separate a direct comparison from an indirect cross-paper comparison. For example, a faster runtime reported on different hardware, input sizes, quality targets, or software versions is not a controlled speed comparison. A structure observed in one assay and a function reported in another are different evidence types.

Weight conclusions by design suitability, bias, measurement validity, uncertainty, independence, and applicability. Do not count positive papers as votes or use citation count, prestige, or model confidence as a scientific-quality score. Identify what evidence would change the synthesis.

## Make the gap precise

Distinguish:

- Knowledge gap: the mechanism, relationship, or outcome remains unresolved.
- Methodological gap: available methods cannot adequately resolve the question.
- Applicability gap: findings do not establish behavior in the target system or regime.
- Evidence conflict: credible results disagree and the cause is unresolved.
- Access or search gap: the present review has not established what evidence exists.

For a proposed gap, state what is known, what is uncertain, why the uncertainty matters, and the discriminating experiment or analysis. Check foundational, recent, contradictory, and adjacent-field work. Prefer bounded wording with a stated search date to an unsupported first-or-only claim.

## Write a synthesis

Organize around claims or mechanisms. For each theme, explain the shared result, meaningful differences, evidence limits, and implication for the user's question. Cite the primary source at the claim it supports. Do not stretch a source beyond the material, conditions, or outcome it actually examines.

Finish with the supported conclusion and the remaining uncertainty that matters for the user's decision. Keep quantitative pooling out of the synthesis unless study compatibility, dependence, estimand, effect measures, and appropriate analysis have been assessed.
