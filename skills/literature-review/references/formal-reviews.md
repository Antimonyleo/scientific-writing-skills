# Formal systematic and scoping reviews

Use when the user requests a review with explicit methods and reproducible coverage. The appropriate discipline-specific methodology and reporting guideline govern; not every research question fits an intervention-effect framework.

## Plan before screening

Specify the question and intended review type, eligibility criteria, information sources, date and language limits with reasons, screening approach, extraction fields, appraisal method, and synthesis plan. Record a protocol or registration where applicable. Document later changes and their reasons; do not retroactively label decisions prespecified.

Select databases and other sources suited to the field. Pilot queries against known eligible studies, preserve full database-specific queries and search dates, and record actual retrieved counts. Search-engine rankings and citation chaining may supplement a review, but do not by themselves establish comprehensive database coverage.

## Track records, reports, and studies

Deduplicate repeated search records, then link related reports to the underlying study or experiment. Retain a mapping so a preprint and journal version, or several outcomes from one cohort, do not become independent studies by accident.

Track title/abstract screening, full texts sought, full texts not obtained, full texts assessed, exclusions with reasons, and included reports and studies. Reconcile flow counts from the actual log. Never generate plausible PRISMA numbers from a narrative description.

For final eligibility decisions, plan independent assessment and disagreement resolution appropriate to the selected methodology. If only one reviewer participated, report that limitation. One model responding under two personas does not establish independent human screening.

## Appraise and synthesize

Choose an appraisal method suited to the study design and the result of interest. Distinguish reporting completeness, risk of bias, and certainty of a body of evidence. Avoid universal evidence hierarchies for all STEM work or a quality score formed by counting reported items.

Preserve outcome definitions, effect measures, uncertainty, and dependence when extracting data. Assess compatibility before pooling. If formal meta-analysis is requested, specify a justified analysis plan and actual computations; a thematic review is not a meta-analysis.

## Report what happened

Use the current applicable reporting guidance and extensions. [PRISMA 2020](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020) addresses systematic-review reporting; the [PRISMA site](https://www.prisma-statement.org/) identifies extensions for other review types, including scoping reviews. Check applicability to the question before choosing a checklist.

Report methods, protocol changes, search coverage, screening, extraction, appraisal, synthesis, limitations, and relevant assistance as actually performed. Do not claim guideline compliance while material steps remain undocumented. A completed checklist or flow diagram does not prove methodological quality or exhaustive retrieval.
