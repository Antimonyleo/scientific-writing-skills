## Task 6 — Competing mechanisms and smallest useful next study

The observation is higher fluorescence after increasing MgCl2 in one preparation. It is not yet a measurement of intended assembly, reproducibility, or assembly rate. The lower predicted free energy is a model result; it neither establishes kinetic accessibility nor validates fluorescence as a structural readout. The hypotheses below were generated after inspecting the observation.

A useful grant aim is: **Determine whether the MgCl2-associated fluorescence increase tracks formation of the intended assemblies or a direct response of the reporter.**

| Candidate mechanism | Key assumption | Prediction that would distinguish it | What would challenge it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1: MgCl2 shifts the system toward the intended assembly, increasing a fluorescence signal coupled to that assembly. | The reporter responds to the intended assembled state; the structural effect and reporter coupling are unchecked premises. | A fluorescence change is accompanied by an increase in intended structures in an independent structural readout, while an assembly-incompetent reporter control shows little corresponding change. | Fluorescence increases without more intended structures, or a matched reporter control shows the same increase. |
| H2: MgCl2 changes reporter brightness or its local environment without increasing the intended assembly. | A direct reporter response under the tested conditions is plausible but unverified. | A matched control that cannot form the intended assembly retains a salt-dependent fluorescence response, and the orthogonal structural readout shows no corresponding increase in intended structures. | Intended structures increase while the control lacks the fluorescence response. |

Both mechanisms could contribute. Fluorescence and structure increasing together would support assembly involvement but would not, by itself, identify the detailed molecular mechanism. Nonspecific aggregation is another explanation to consider if the structural readout reveals it.

The smallest useful next study is a controlled feasibility pilot on a new, independently prepared sample, split into matched low- and high-MgCl2 conditions using the original salt levels. Record the exact concentrations before running it. Keep DNA concentration, temperature, acquisition settings, and dilution matched; use a volume-matched sham addition for the comparison condition.

- Collect fluorescence traces before and after the salt or sham addition. These give the time course of the signal, not automatically assembly kinetics.
- Run a matched reporter control that cannot form the intended assembly, with its suitability checked for the actual reporter chemistry. A free fluorophore alone may not reproduce the reporter’s local environment and may be insufficient.
- Measure intended structures at matched endpoints with an orthogonal readout capable of distinguishing intended particles from unassembled material and aggregates. Imaging is a candidate if suitable access and resolution are available; choose the method after checking those constraints.
- Treat the new preparation as the independent unit. Split aliquots, time points, and fields of view are within-preparation measurements. A single pilot preparation can test readout feasibility but cannot establish reproducibility. Use pilot variability, a meaningful target effect, and the planned inference to determine how many independent preparations a subsequent study needs.

Record predictions and interpretation rules before inspecting the new outcomes. If fluorescence tracks increased intended structures and the control remains stable, advance toward an assembly mechanism study. If the control changes similarly without a structural change, prioritize reporter calibration or replacement. If both effects occur, separate their contributions before using fluorescence quantitatively. If the readout fails or outcomes are ambiguous, revise the assay rather than treating that failure as evidence against assembly.

The most consequential unknowns are the reporter’s coupling to structure and access to a suitable orthogonal readout.
